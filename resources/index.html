YOUR STUDENT FIELD GUIDE

Science has a language.
You can learn it.

Practical guidance for reading research, comparing scientific approaches, and asking thoughtful questions.

01Choosing a research approach+

Wet lab research uses physical experiments with materials or living systems. Computational work analyzes data or builds models. Theoretical research develops mathematical or conceptual explanations. Field research gathers observations in real-world settings. Many research questions combine several approaches.

Explore Research Faculty →
02How to read a faculty or lab website+

Start with the big questions, then look for methods, people, and publications. Distinguish the faculty member’s official appointment from a lab’s research themes. Use original sources to understand how an individual project fits into the broader field.

Explore Research Faculty →
03How to approach a paper+

Read the abstract, examine the figures, and identify the main question. Write down what the authors measured or calculated, what the evidence supports, and what remains uncertain. Check the DOI or publisher page for the publication version and date.

Explore a scholarly paper →
04Keep a research notebook+

Record the question you are exploring, the sources you read, unfamiliar terms, and ideas to revisit. Separate the authors’ conclusions from your own interpretations. A short comparison of two papers is often more useful than a long list of links.

Explore Research Faculty →
05Prepare for a research conversation+

Choose a specific detail from a faculty member’s work. Explain your interest in a few clear sentences and ask one focused question. A suggestion for something to read next can be a useful starting point.

Build an introduction →
06Share a scientific idea+

A clear presentation connects a question, an approach, the evidence, and its limits. The Technology & Science Undergraduate Research Forum is one UT setting for learning how students communicate science. Explore its official site with your research mentor.
